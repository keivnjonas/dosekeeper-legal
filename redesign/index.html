14Support Blog — Common Questions
My reminders aren't arriving

1. In DoseKeeper Settings, confirm Push reorder alerts and Dose reminders are both on.
2. In iOS Settings → Notifications → DoseKeeper, confirm notifications are allowed.
3. Changed devices recently? Toggle push off and back on to refresh the device registration.
4. Check your phone's timezone — schedules are anchored to Settings → Profile.

I logged a dose by mistake

On Today, long-press the dose row and choose Mark pending. If you used Mark all, an Undo toast sits at the bottom of the screen for 5 seconds.

I can't find an old dose

History shows the last 90 days. For longer windows — or to hand something to a doctor — use Export CSV at the bottom of History (30 / 90 / 365 day ranges).

How do I let a family member help me reorder?

1. Settings → Caregiving → Generate code, then pick a preset: Caregiver, Orderer, Clinician, or Minimal.
2. Send the code. They sign up, then Settings → Caregiving → Redeem code.
3. Tune what they see in Settings → Who can help → [their name].

How do I delete my account?

Email K2@jonasenterprises.com from the address you signed up with. We delete your profile, supplements, dose history, schedules, trips, caretaker links and push subscriptions within 14 days.

15Privacy, in one paragraph

Pitch notes — what each 2006 element is doing for us in 2026

The bet: every module on a MySpace profile is a job a modern marketing site already has, just named more honestly. Nothing here is decoration for its own sake — each numbered block maps to a page we'd have built anyway.

  • 1 Profile identity. Name, one-line positioning, location, "Last Ship" date. Replaces the hero — and the ship date does the work a "trusted by" logo wall usually fakes.
  • 2 Contact table. Eight conversion actions above the fold in 120px of height. Email, share, mailing list, bookmark, book a call, bug report, download, review.
  • 3 URL box. The 2006 share primitive. Now: canonical link + copy-to-clipboard, which is what people actually want when they're texting a recommendation.
  • 4 Interests table. Our spec sheet. Platforms, values, what we're building now. Scannable in five seconds, no scroll-triggered reveals.
  • 5 Extended network banner. Social proof slot. Swap in the real number once we agree what we're willing to publish.
  • 6 Latest blog entry. Changelog on the homepage. The strongest signal a small studio has: the thing was updated this month.
  • 7 Blurbs. "About us" and "Who we'd like to meet" — the second one is our ICP, stated out loud, which most sites bury in a deck.
  • 8 Product Space (Top 8). Feature grid as a friends list. Ranked, finite, and it forces us to pick eight.
  • 9 Friend comments. Testimonials in reverse-chron with dates. Marked SAMPLE — needs real, permissioned quotes before this goes anywhere near production.
  • 10 Browse filters. Platform / price / status. Honest about what's shipping vs. planned.
  • 11 Featured product. Feature table pulled straight from the support docs, so it can't drift from what the app does.
  • 12 More products. Roadmap as a grid. Each tile carries its own status label.
  • 13 Support contact. One email, one expectation ("1–2 business days"). No ticket portal.
  • 14 Support blog. The five real questions from SUPPORT.md, verbatim, indexable.
  • 15 Privacy in one paragraph. The differentiator: no ad SDKs, no third-party analytics. Say it on the page, not just in the policy.

Yellow-highlighted text = placeholder that needs a real value before build. Type: Verdana 11px throughout, Arial Black for shouts, Courier for URLs — all system faces, which is what makes this read as 2006 rather than as a filter applied to a modern page.

9Jonas Brothers' Blurbs
About us:

Hello! We're Kevin, Joe and Nick and this is our MySpace baby. We started this band in Wyckoff, New Jersey in 2005, and twenty years later we put out Greetings from Your Hometown — our seventh record. Thanks for being here since the beginning, or since about four minutes ago. Both count.

Who we'd like to meet:

Everyone in row 400. The people who still have the 2006 CD in a car that no longer has a CD player. Anyone bringing a homemade sign.

Pitch notes — what each 2006 module is doing for us

The argument: this band was made on MySpace in 2006, and a MySpace profile already contains every module a modern artist site needs — it just names them more bluntly. Nothing below is nostalgia for its own sake; each numbered block is a page we'd build anyway.

  • 1 Profile identity. Name, tagline, hometown, last login. Replaces the hero video — and "Wyckoff, New Jersey" does more brand work than any full-bleed photo.
  • 2 Contact table. Eight actions in 120px: message, share, follow, bookmark, mailing list, fan club, rate. Every conversion sits above the fold.
  • 3 URL box. The 2006 share primitive. Now: canonical link plus copy-to-clipboard, which is what people actually want when they're texting a friend.
  • 4 The music player. The single most valuable pixel on the page. Autoplay was the point in 2006; today it's a streaming-link hub with the new single on top.
  • 5 Band Details. The press-kit table. Members, genre, label, latest album, current tour — scannable in five seconds by a journalist or a promoter.
  • 6 Extended network banner. Social proof slot. Swap the number for whatever we're comfortable publishing.
  • 7 Upcoming Shows. Tour dates on the homepage, not one click in. This is the revenue module — it should never require navigation.
  • 8 Latest blog entry. Announcements in the band's own voice, above the fold, dated. The thing every artist site buries under a "News" tab.
  • 9 Blurbs. "About us" and "Who we'd like to meet." The second one is audience positioning stated out loud — most sites hide that in a deck.
  • 10 Top 8. Members and tour guests as a ranked, finite grid. It forces a hierarchy, which a logo carousel never does.
  • 11 Friend comments. Fan wall in reverse-chron. Marked SAMPLE — needs real, permissioned posts before this goes anywhere near production.
  • 12 Persistent player. Carries across screens so audio survives navigation. In 2006 that was an accident; here it's the spec.
  • 13 Latest release. One record, one date, three actions: listen, buy, watch.
  • 14 Discography grid. Twenty years in one screen, chronological. The 2006 sleeve sitting next to the 2025 one is the pitch.
  • 15 Date filters. Country, month, guest, status. What people actually search a tour page for.
  • 16 Full routing. Opening and closing nights called out; sold-out states shown rather than hidden.
  • 17 Before You Go. Doors, bag policy, signs, resale. The support content that otherwise arrives as 400 identical DMs.

Accuracy: album, tour name, opening and closing nights, guest acts and the new single are real and current. Anything highlighted yellow — view counts, TBA rows, exact routing — is placeholder and needs the real data. Fan comments are invented and labelled as such. Type: Verdana 11px throughout, Arial Black for shouts, Courier for URLs and dates — all system faces, which is what makes this read as 2006 rather than as a filter over a modern page.
